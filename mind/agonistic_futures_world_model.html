◆ NODES

⚡ Data Centres

The physical compute substrate. Energy demand nearing aviation sector levels (IEA 2024). Water withdrawal for cooling in drought-prone regions (Li et al. 2023). E-waste from accelerated hardware churn projected at millions of metric tons/year by 2030 (Wang et al. 2024).

💧 Water / Energy Commons

Shared resource pools squeezed by AI buildout. Grid capacity conflicts (Maryland Piedmont, 2024). Municipal water competition in AZ, OR, Bengaluru, Santiago. Agonistic flashpoints where material scarcity meets political mobilisation.

👷 Labour Markets

GenAI displacing cognitive/routine work (NBER). Marginalised workers face greatest precarity. Automation frontier expands faster than retraining ecosystems. Distributional effects remain contested — a live agonistic cleavage.

📱 Algorithmic Public Sphere

AI-mediated information ecosystems eroding Arendtian public deliberation. Feedback loops between algorithmic curation and training data. Bias amplification documented in hiring, lending, criminal justice (ACL 2024).

⛏️ Mineral Supply Chains

Critical minerals (Li, Co, Ga, Ge, rare earths) under geopolitical tension. US DOE 2023 assessment flags supply-chain concentration. Semiconductor fab environmental damage. Agonistic arena for resource sovereignty claims.

🏛️ Governance Regimes

Nation-state regulation (grid permits, emissions caps, e-waste directives). Multi-stakeholder bodies. Must shift from technocratic consensus to agonistic pluralism — institutionalising conflict as productive rather than suppressing it (Popa & Blok 2020).

⚔️ Agonistic Arena

The central political space where adversary relations play out. Counter-hegemonic contestation, resource sovereignty claims, public deliberation over AI's material costs. Mouffe's agonistic pluralism applied to human-AI interdependence.

◆ EDGE LEGEND

Human → Agentic

Labour supply, attention, democratic regulation, training data. These flows constitute AI's social substrate — the human activity that fuels both the economic and epistemic machinery.

Agentic → Human

Automation of cognitive/routine labour, algorithmic curation of public discourse, feedback-loop bias. AI exerts structural power back onto human societies, shaping opportunity and information.

Human → Material

Extraction rights, land-use permits, grid expansion approvals, water allocation policy. Human governance regimes mediate access to the physical resources AI requires.

Material → Human

Resource scarcity signals, pollution burdens, e-waste contamination, climate feedback. The material world imposes costs and constraints back on human communities — unevenly distributed.

AI → Material

Data centre energy and water demand, hardware manufacturing waste, mineral depletion. AI's physical metabolism is the primary driver of the material conflicts modelled.

Material → AI

Geopolitical bottlenecks in Ga, Ge, rare earths; fab capacity constraints; thermal limits. Material shortages and supply-chain weaponisation throttle AI's expansion trajectory.

AGONISTIC ARENA — The adversarial political space where these seven clusters interact. Conflict is not a bug to be engineered away but the constitutive condition of human-AI interdependence (Mouffe, 2013; Popa & Blok, 2020). Legitimate governance institutions must institutionalise agonistic contestation — enabling adversary relations without collapsing into antagonism.
